# Flanger
> One short sweeping delay, and inverted feedback for the hollow version.

A single delay of a few milliseconds, swept, mixed back with the dry. The comb it makes is the whole sound, and the feedback decides how sharp the teeth of that comb are.

## The controls

- **rate** - the sweep, as a note value.
- **depth** - how far the delay moves. This sets how far the notches travel, which is heard as the range of the sweep.
- **feedback** - up to 0.95. Low is a gentle whoosh; high is the jet plane.
- **negative** *(extra)* - the feedback goes back in inverted. The notches land in different places and the result is hollow rather than resonant - the two classic flanger sounds are these two settings, and most units only give you one.
- **spread** - how far out of phase the two channels sweep.
- **mix** - wet against dry. At exactly half it is the deepest, because a comb needs equal parts to cancel completely.

## Using it well

**Half mix, not full.** A flanger at full wet has nothing to interfere with and loses most of its character. The notches come from the dry and the delayed copy cancelling, so the effect is strongest when they are equal.

**Negative feedback on a chord.** Positive feedback emphasises the harmonics that sit on the peaks; negative removes the ones that sit in the notches. On a sustained chord the second is much more dramatic and far less obviously an effect.

**A slow rate on a whole mix.** At a one-bar rate and a small depth it is a movement you feel rather than hear, which is a good use on a send.
