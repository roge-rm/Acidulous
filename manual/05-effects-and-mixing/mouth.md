# Mouth
> Diction's throat on any track: it shapes whatever goes through into a vowel.

## The controls

- **vowel** - oo, oh, ah, eh, ee from bottom to top.
- **size** - the throat's size, big and dark to small and bright.
- **nasal** - opens the nose.
- **move** *(extra)* - what moves the vowel: **lfo** on the tempo, the track's
  own **level** (louder opens the mouth), or another track through the
  **sidechain** (**key**).
- **rate** - the LFO's note value.
- **depth** - how far the vowel moves from where it's set.
- **mix** - the dry sound against the vowel.
- **gain** - the level out.
- **sidechain** - the track that moves it when **move** is **key**.
- **words** *(extra)* - how far a clip's words move the mouth. See below.

## Words

Turn **words** up and the track gets a **words** lane in the editor, whatever
machine it is. Give its notes words there, as for Diction, and the mouth says
each note's word: the vowel held while the note is down, the sounds before it
on the way in, and the ones after it when you let go. A P or a T shuts the
mouth for a moment, an S hisses and an M goes through the nose. Between words
the mouth goes back to **move**. The words only reach a Mouth in a track's
inserts, not in a group or the master.

## Tips

- On a synth lead with **move** at **level**, harder notes say "ah".
- Point the sidechain at a drum loop and a pad talks in its rhythm.
- Slow **lfo** sweeps from oo to ah sound like a wah played by a voice.
- A bright, buzzy sound says words best: a saw pad or a chord with plenty
  of top. Short, open words come through clearest.
