# Tremolo
> Amplitude on an LFO, and the same lever turned into an auto-pan.

The oldest effect there is, and one the rack was missing entirely. Rate, depth and a shape to sweep with - and then one control that turns it into a different effect without adding a second one.

## The controls

- **rate** - the note value it pulses on.
- **depth** - how far down each dip goes. At full the sound stops between pulses.
- **shape** - sine, triangle or square. Square is the chopped, gated sound; sine is the amplifier's own vibrato.
- **pan** *(extra)* - not a second effect but the same LFO arriving at the two channels further and further out of step. At nothing both channels duck together and it is a tremolo; at full they are opposite and it is an auto-pan; and everywhere between is the wobble neither has a name for.
- **skew** *(extra)* - bends the waveform's duty, so the dip can be a stab (fast down, slow up) or a swell (slow down, fast up).
- **mix** - wet against dry, which on a tremolo sets how far the dips go rather than how present the effect is.

## Using it well

**Skew turns it into a rhythm.** A square shape with the skew hard over is a gate pattern rather than a wobble, and at a sixteenth rate it makes a held chord into a part.

**The middle of the pan control is the interesting place.** Fully out of phase is a familiar auto-pan. A third of the way is a sound that moves without ever clearly being on one side, which is much harder to get any other way.

**On a send it is a rhythmic room**, because what pulses is the returned effect and the dry stays steady underneath.
