# Spectral
> The track taken apart into its frequencies and put back together.

About forty times a second, Spectral splits the sound into its frequencies,
changes them, and puts it back. With nothing turned, what comes out is what
went in.

## The controls

- **freeze** *(extra)* - **hold** keeps the sound as it is at that moment,
  a chord or a vowel that goes on for as long as it's held. **run** lets go.
- **blur** - lets each frequency fade instead of stopping, so notes run into
  each other. At the top, a note trails for seconds.
- **smear** - scrambles the timing within each slice, for a washed, distant,
  whispered sound.
- **robot** - throws the timing away, for a flat, buzzing voice.
- **peaks** *(extra)* - keeps only the strongest frequencies, a glassy,
  whistling sound. Further up, fewer are left.
- **tilt** - leans everything darker or brighter, turning about 1 kHz.
- **mix** - the dry track against the changed one.
- **gain** - the level out.

The changed sound runs about 40 ms behind the track. The dry sound in
**mix** is held back to match, so the two stay in step.

## Tips

- Map **freeze** to a button and hold a chord under the next part.
- **Robot Voice** on a sung or spoken track is the classic flat robot.
- **Ice** on a pad or a piano leaves a glassy shimmer of its loudest notes.
