# Transient
> The start of each hit and the ring after it, up or down.

Shapes each hit by its own movement rather than its level, so there's no
threshold to set: a quiet hit and a loud one are shaped the same.

## The controls

- **attack** - up brings the start of each hit forward, for snap and crack.
  Down softens it, as if it were further away.
- **sustain** - up brings out the ring after each hit, the room and the body.
  Down tightens it and dries it out.
- **time** - how long counts as the start of a hit and as its ring. Short
  catches only the click, long the whole hit.
- **split** *(extra)* - only what's above this frequency is shaped. At the
  bottom of its range the whole sound is.
- **gain** - the output level.

### Grid

- **grid** *(extra)* - shapes each hit by where it lands in the bar, so a
  flat drum loop gets a groove. The row of steps under the knobs sets each
  step up, flat or down: tap one to go flat, up, down. A hit on an up step
  gets more attack, on a down step less. The step is the one the hit starts
  in, and it follows the song's position and the track's swing. Only while
  the song plays. In a very fast roll on a drum that rings, some hits play
  flat.
- **depth** - how much an up or down step adds or takes, in dB.
- **rate** - how long a step is: 1/16 triplet, 1/16 or 1/8.
- **length** - how many steps before the row starts over.

## Tips

- On a drum loop, set **split** to 1 or 2 kHz and turn up **attack**: the
  hats and snare snap and the kick stays as it was.
- Turning **attack** up raises the peaks, so turn **gain** down to match.
- **sustain** down on a roomy recording takes the room out.
- With **grid** on, leave **attack** at 0 and set only a few steps: up on 2
  and 4 and the snare cracks while the rest stays as it was. Try
  **Backbeat Snap** or **Ghost Hats**.
