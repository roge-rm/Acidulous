# Eq
> Three bands, and a tilt that trades top for bottom on one knob.

A low shelf, a sweepable bell in the middle and a high shelf: the shape every mixing desk has had for fifty years, because it is the one that matches how people describe sound.

## The controls

- **lowgain** and **lowfreq** - a shelf up to 15 dB either way, with its corner anywhere from 40 to 500 Hz.
- **midgain**, **midfreq** and **midq** - the bell. `midq` runs from broad (0.3) to surgical (6), which is the difference between a tone change and removing one ringing frequency.
- **highgain** and **highfreq** - the top shelf, from 1.5 to 16 kHz.
- **tilt** *(extra)* - one knob that lifts the top while dropping the bottom, or the reverse. Not a fourth band: it is a single lever across the whole spectrum, which is how somebody actually decides a sound is too dark or too thin.
- **gain** - the output trim, because boosting three bands makes something louder and that is not the same as making it better.

## Using it well

**Cut before you boost.** A mid bell pulled down 4 dB at the frequency that is in the way does more for a mix than the same 4 dB added somewhere else, and it costs no headroom.

**A narrow Q is for a problem, not for a tone.** If something rings, sweep `midfreq` with `midgain` up and `midq` high until it gets worse, then pull the gain down. If you are trying to make a sound brighter, do the opposite: a broad Q and a small move.

**Tilt is the fastest way to audition a decision.** Before reaching for two shelves, try a little tilt: it usually turns out that "needs more top" meant "needs less bottom", and one control answers that question in a second.
