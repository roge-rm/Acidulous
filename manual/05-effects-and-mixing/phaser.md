# Phaser
> Allpass notches sweeping, from two stages to eight.

Cousin to the flanger and not the same thing: a flanger's notches are evenly spaced because they come from a delay, a phaser's are not because they come from a chain of allpass filters. That irregular spacing is why a phaser sounds like it is moving through a sound rather than over it.

## The controls

- **rate** - the sweep, as a note value.
- **depth** - how far the notches travel.
- **feedback** - up to 0.9, sharpening the notches into resonances.
- **stages** - two, four, six or eight. Each pair of stages is another notch: two is subtle and vocal, eight is thick and obviously an effect.
- **spread** *(extra)* - the two channels sweep out of phase with each other by this much, so the notches are in different places left and right. At full it is very wide and slightly disorienting, which on a pad is exactly right.
- **mix** - wet against dry.

## Using it well

**Stages decide the character before anything else does.** Do not reach for depth and feedback to make a phaser more dramatic - reach for stages. Four is the classic pedal; eight is a studio unit.

**Feedback is the difference between a phaser and a filter sweep.** With none, the notches are shallow and it is a gentle movement. With plenty, they ring, and the sweep becomes the loudest thing in the part.

**Slow and shallow on a send.** A phaser at a two-bar rate under a mix is one of the few effects that adds movement without anybody being able to say what changed.
