# Vessel
> Plays the track through a body of wood, skin, stone, metal, glass or a bell.

Vessel treats the track as something hitting a body, and you hear the body
ring. A drum loop through a wooden box knocks, a bass line through a bell
chimes.

## The body

- **material** - glass, wood, metal, skin, stone, bell, plate or tube. Each
  has its own set of overtones. The high ones of wood and stone die fast,
  metal and glass ring on.
- **size** - the body's lowest note.
- **key** and **scale** - snap the size to the nearest note of a scale.
  **off** leaves it free.
- **follow** *(extra)* - tunes the body to the last note the track played,
  so it rings along with the part. Only on a track's own inserts, since a
  send or a group doesn't get notes.

## The ring

- **decay** - how long the lowest note rings, 0.05 to 20 s.
- **damp** - how much sooner the higher notes die.

## The touch

- **strike** - where the track lands on the body. **pickup** - where it's
  heard from, with left and right a little apart. Moving either changes
  which overtones you hear.
- **drive** - pushes the track into the body through a soft clip, so loud
  hits bark. It adds grit, not level.

## Out

- **mix** - the dry track against the body.
- **gain** - the level out.

A body tuned to the notes playing through it can't get much louder than the
track, so **follow** and a long **decay** are safe to try.

## Tips

- On drums, set **key** and **scale** to the song's and every hit gets a
  pitch in the key.
- **skin** with a low **size** and some **drive** makes thin drums bigger.
- **follow** with **glass** or **bell** on a lead puts a ring under each note.
