# Tine

> Modelled bars, tines and pans: marimba, vibraphone, xylophone,
> glockenspiel, thumb piano, music box, steel pan, handpan, tongue drum,
> chimes, crotales, gamelan, gong, singing bowl, slit drum, temple blocks,
> cowbell and triangle.

Tine doesn't play recordings. Each note is a handful of resonances tuned the
way the thing struck is tuned. A marimba bar is carved away underneath so its
first overtone sits two octaves up and the next two octaves and a third; a
xylophone's is carved to a twelfth; a glockenspiel's steel bar is left as it
is, its overtones far from any chord. A thumb piano's tine is held at one
end, which puts its overtones higher still, and a pan's note is hammered
until it rings its octave and twelfth.

What strikes it matters as much. A soft mallet stays on the bar for a few
milliseconds and can only set the note going; a hard one is gone before the
overtones start, so they ring out.

## Bar

- **model** - **marimba**, **vibes**, **xylophone**, **glock**, **thumb**
  (piano), **music box**, **steel pan**, **handpan**, **tongue drum**,
  **chimes** (tubular bells), **crotales**, **saron** and **bonang** (gamelan),
  **gong**, **bowl** (singing bowl), **slit drum**, **temple block**,
  **cowbell** or **triangle**. A balafon is the marimba with **buzz** up.
- **tune** - in cents.
- **mallet** - how hard the mallet, finger or pin: soft yarn to bare metal.
  Playing harder makes it a little harder too.
- **position** - where it's struck. In the middle a bar's second overtone
  stays quiet; towards the end the note thins and the overtones come up.
- **decay** - how long the notes ring. Low notes always ring longer.
- **bright** - how much of the overtones, and how long they last.
- **damp** - the damper bar: how much a note stops when you let it go. The
  sustain pedal lifts it.

## Body

- **tube** - the tube under each bar, ringing along with the note (and its
  odd overtones). On the pans and the tongue drum it's the shell.
- **motor** - the discs turning in a vibraphone's tubes, in turns a second.
  At 0 they stand still.
- **depth** - how much the turning discs pulse the note. The mod wheel adds
  to it.
- **bloom** - on the pans, how much the octave grows out of the note after
  it's struck.
- **buzz** - a rattle that touches a tine when it swings wide.

## Play

- **rolls** - held notes struck again and again, this many times a second,
  never quite evenly. At 0 a note is struck once.
- **spread** - low notes to the left, high to the right.
- **voices**, **velocity**, **octave**, **bend** and **volume**.

Pressure (from the keyboard or each note's own, with MPE) draws a bow across
a held bar: the note swells up and sustains for as long as you press.

## Tips

- **Vibraphone** stops each note when you let it go; hold the sustain pedal
  to let them ring, as a vibraphone player does.
- **Vibes Motor** and **Slow Motor** turn the discs; the mod wheel deepens
  the pulse.
- **Marimba Roll**, **Vibes Roll** and **Pan Roll** need held notes.
