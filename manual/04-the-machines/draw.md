# Draw

> Modelled free reeds: harmonicas, accordions, melodica, harmonium and the
> reeds that sound through pipes.

Draw doesn't play recordings. Each note is a reed, a thin tongue of metal in
a slot, blown by the air pressing on it. It starts slowly, settles on its
note and stops when the air does, the way a real reed does. Blow harder and
it gets brighter and louder while the pitch hardly moves.

The accordion, harmonium, melodica and harmonicas were fitted to recordings
of real ones, and the bandoneon to the one recording there was.

## Reeds

- **model** - the instrument. The harmonicas (**diatonic**, **chromatic**,
  **tremolo harp**, **octave harp**), **accordion**, **bandoneon**,
  **concertina**, **melodica**, **harmonium**, the reeds in pipes
  (**sheng**, **shō**, **khaen**) and the **pitch pipe**.
- **tune** - in cents.
- **set** - how far each reed rests from its slot. Closer speaks sooner and
  brighter, and chokes sooner when blown hard.
- **chamber** - the size of the space behind each reed. It changes how the
  reed starts and the colour of the note.
- **register** - which reeds each note sounds, as an accordion's register
  switches do. They're named by footage: 8′ sounds the note, 16′ an octave
  down and 4′ an octave up, and up to three 8′ reeds can sound together.
  **auto** is the instrument's own: two 8′ on an accordion, 16′ and 8′ on a
  bandoneon, 8′ and 4′ on a harmonium, one 8′ on the rest.
- **detune** - how far a note's 8′ reeds are tuned apart, in cents. At 0
  they agree (dry); 10 to 15 beats gently (tremolo); 25 and more beats fast
  (a wet musette). With three, one sits on the note and the others either
  side. On a harmonium, two 8′ reeds a little apart are its celeste.
- **cassotto** - the 16′ and the first 8′ reeds speak into a tone chamber,
  which makes them mellower.
- **high**, **low** - where the register's 4′ and 16′ ranks sound, in
  semitones: an octave up and down to start with, or any interval, as an
  organ's mutation stops. A 4′ moved to +7 gives every note a bare fifth;
  +16 a tenth. They move the sheng's and khaen's extra pipes too.

Reeds never quite agree, so a note on two or three reeds beats, each
harmonic at its own speed. That's most of what makes an accordion sound like
one.

## Left hand

Keys below **below** in the **left hand** group play an accordion's bass
buttons: each key sounds its bass note, low and on two reeds an octave apart,
and its chord in the middle of the keyboard, on reeds of their own. Any key of
the same name plays the same bass and chord, as on the buttons. **chord** is
**major**, **minor**, **7th**, **dim** or **none**, the bass alone. **level**
is the left hand against the right. At **off** every key plays the right hand.
It works on the accordion, bandoneon, concertina, melodica and harmonium, not
the harmonicas or the pipes.

## Harmonicas

A harmonica hole has two reeds, one for blowing and one for drawing, with
your mouth behind them. Draw models the mouth too: where your tongue sits
changes the note, which is how a player bends.

- **key** - the harp's key, G up to F♯. A diatonic harp has every note of
  its key and bends to most of the rest.
- **playing** - **player** puts each note where a player would find it on
  the harp: on a hole as it is, or bent down from the hole above it. Notes a
  player can't bend to, overblows among them, sound on a reed of their own.
  **straight** plays every note on a reed of its own, so none are bent.

The chromatic harp's reeds each sit behind a valve, so every note is a reed
of its own. A tremolo harp sounds two reeds a note, tuned apart by
**detune**; an octave harp sounds the note and the octave above.

Pitch bend down on a diatonic harp played like a player bends the note with
your tongue, as far as its hole will go: a lot on holes 2 and 3, a little on
the others. Bend up, and on the notes that don't bend, moves the note
instead.

## Pipes

The sheng, shō and khaen sound each reed into a bamboo pipe, which rings
with it and takes most of its edge off: they're close to pure. The khaen's
reeds are heard in its wind chest too, so it's buzzier.

On the shō, **playing** set to **player** gives each key its chord
(aitake) where the shō has one, at the shō's own pitch, tuned in fifths
from A: A, B, C, C♯, D, E, F♯, G, G♯ and the high A each have one. The
other keys, and **straight**, play single pipes. Its pipes are never
filed quite alike, so its chords shimmer.

The register works on the sheng and khaen as well: 4′ adds the octave above.

## Breath

- **pressure** - how hard the reeds are blown, from gently to hard. Velocity
  and pressure from your keyboard blow harder still.
- **attack** - how long the breath or bellows take to come up, in ms.
- **release** - how long they take to stop, in ms.
- **air** - the air you hear: the breath, and the turbulence of the air
  through each reed. No breath or bellows is quite even, and the reeds wander
  a little with it.
- **shake** - the bellows turned back and forth, turns a second, as an
  accordion player shakes them. Each turn the sound dips and the other set
  of reeds speaks. Only on the accordion, bandoneon and concertina.
- **sync** - turns the bellows on the song's grid instead: quarters,
  eighths, triplets, sixteenths and so on, in time with the song.
- **from track** - another track's sound blows the reeds: they speak as
  loud as it plays and fall silent when it stops. Hold the notes and let
  the other track play them in.

The accordion, bandoneon, concertina and harmonium share one bellows, so a
big chord lowers its pressure a little.

Pressure added while a note is held, from aftertouch or a key that sends its
own, makes that note louder and brighter.

## Hands

- **cup** - your hands round the harp, from open to closed. Closed is dark
  and hollow. The mod wheel closes them further, for a wah.
- **vibrato** - a throat vibrato. Like a player's, it's mostly in the level
  and no two cycles are quite alike.
- **rate** - its speed, in Hz.
- **words** - how much a clip's words shape a harmonica, as a talking-harp
  player does. Type words into the notes as you would for Diction: the
  vowels colour the sound and move the player's tongue, so a note that can
  bend bends with them, and m, b, p and w close the hands.

## Out

- **voices** - how many notes at once, up to eight.
- **velocity** - how much playing harder blows harder.
- **octave**, **bend** - the octave, and the pitch bend range in semitones.
- **volume**.

## Macros

The **play** page has eight macro knobs. A patch can name them (Squeeze,
Musette, etc.) and the names show on the knobs, on automation lanes and in the
controller mapping list. They sit at zero when a patch loads, which is the
patch as it was made.

The **macros** page sets what each one does. A macro has four routes, a
destination (**to**) and an amount (**by**, from -1 to 1) for each. Turning the
knob moves the destination by knob times amount, across that control's whole
range at 1. Pitch moves the whole instrument, 24 semitones at 1. The model,
the register, the switches and stepped choices and the tuning can't be
reached. A change to the attack, release, velocity, **set**, **chamber** or
**detune** is heard from the next note. Tap a macro's name to rename it.
