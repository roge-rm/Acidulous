# Fret

> Modelled electric guitars and basses: strings, pickups, a player's hands
> and an amp loud enough to feed back.

Fret doesn't play recordings. Each note is a string, plucked by a pick, a
finger or a slapping thumb, and heard the way an electric guitar hears it,
through a magnet sitting under the string. A pickup
hears the string where it sits. Near the neck it hears a round, full note;
by the bridge, a thin bright one; and a quarter of the way along it can't
hear the 4th harmonic at all.

## Guitar

- **model** - **guitar**, **12-string** (the low four courses with an
  octave string, the top two doubled), **baritone** (a longer neck, tuned
  down), **hollowbody** (a jazz archtop, warm and shorter), **steel** (a lap
  or pedal steel, long and pure: use the slide), **bass**, **5-string** and
  **fretless** bass.
- **tune** - in cents.
- **pickup** - **neck**, **both** or **bridge**.
- **coil** - **single** (bright and clear) or **humbucker** (two coils side
  by side: thicker and darker).
- **tone** - the guitar's tone knob: the coil's resonance falls as you turn
  it down.
- **sustain** - how long the strings ring.
- **bright** - how long they keep their top end. On the fretless it also runs from a dark thump to the mwah.
- **ring** - how long a string keeps sounding after you let go: at 0 your
  fingers stop it, all the way up it rings out like a held note.

## Hands

- **stroke** - **pick**, **finger** or **slap** (the thumb snapping the
  string against the frets).
- **hardness** - a soft or hard pick or finger.
- **position** - where along the string it's struck: near the bridge is
  thin, further out is warmer.
- **swell** - each note fades in from silence over this many seconds, as a
  player rolls the volume knob up after picking. At 0 it starts at once.
- **mute** - the palm resting on the strings by the bridge. The mod wheel
  does the same, so you can mute as you play.
- **harmonic** - a finger touching the **12**th, **7**th or **5**th fret,
  for a bell-like harmonic an octave, a twelfth or two octaves up.
- **buzz** - lets the strings slap against the frets when played hard.
- **slide** - with one voice, a new note slides from the last instead of
  being picked again.
- **vibrato** - a finger rocking on the string. Pressure adds to it.
- **strum** - plays a chord across the strings, this many milliseconds
  between strings, instead of all at once.
- **direction** - strum **down** (low string first), **up** or **both**
  ways in turn.

## Amp

- **drive** - the amp, from clean to flat out, with a speaker coming in as it
  does.
- **feedback** - how much the amp's sound shakes the strings back. With
  drive up, a held note sustains and can jump up to a harmonic.
- **voices**, **velocity**, **octave**, **bend** and **volume**.

## Macros

The **play** page has eight macro knobs. A patch can name them (Grit, Palm,
etc.) and the names show on the knobs, on automation lanes and in the
controller mapping list. They sit at zero when a patch loads, which is the
patch as it was made.

The **macros** page sets what each one does. A macro has four routes, a
destination (**to**) and an amount (**by**, from -1 to 1) for each. Turning the
knob moves the destination by knob times amount, across that control's whole
range at 1. Pitch moves the whole instrument, 24 semitones at 1. The model,
the switches and stepped choices and the tuning can't be reached. A change to
the hardness, position, strum, slide, velocity or swell is heard from the next
note. Tap a macro's name to rename it.

## Tips

- **Chug** and **Funk** use **mute**; try moving the mod wheel as you play to
  open the notes up.
- **Singing Lead** and **Feedback Swell** need held notes: let them ring and
  the amp takes over.
- For strummed chords, play all the notes at once and let **strum** spread
  them.
- Put Fret through the **Amp** effect for a bigger amp and cabinet than its
  own.
