# Timber

> Modelled woodwinds: reeds and flutes.

Timber is the woodwind version of Brazen. A woodwind is either a reed vibrating
against a mouthpiece or a jet of air blown across an edge, feeding a tube with
holes in it.

## The family

**family** picks the kind of instrument:

- **reed** - clarinet and saxophone.
- **double reed** - oboe and bassoon.
- **air jet** - flute. **aim** sets how the air hits the edge, like a flute
  player's lips.

## The tube

- **bore** - cylindrical or conical. A cylinder gives the hollow clarinet sound;
  a cone sounds more like a sax.
- **body** and **bell** - the shape of the end.
- **loss** - how much energy is lost in the tube.
- **fingering**, **below** and **answer** - the tone holes, including the part of
  the tube past the note you're playing.
- **fork** - cross-fingerings, which sound slightly different from normal
  fingerings, as on a real instrument.

## Blowing

**pressure** and **breath** work as on Brazen. The flute family adds **jet** and
**aim**, and **tongue** adds articulation.

## Tips

- Pressure decides the register. Overblow a clarinet and it jumps up a twelfth
  rather than an octave, like the real one.
- The breath noise is a big part of the realism.
- Use **tongue** between notes so a phrase doesn't sound like one long note.
