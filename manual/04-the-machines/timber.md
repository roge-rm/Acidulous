# Timber

> Woodwinds by modelling: a reed or a jet of air, a tube, and holes down the side of it.

Timber is the woodwind counterpart to Brazen. Where brass is lips against a tube, a woodwind is either a **reed** beating against a mouthpiece or a **jet** of air across an edge - and then a tube with holes in it.

## The family

**family** chooses reed (clarinet, saxophone), double reed (oboe, bassoon) or air jet (flute). They are genuinely different mechanisms, not three filter settings:

- a reed's table is **a curve**, not a switch - it closes gradually as the pressure rises, and that curve is the difference between a clarinet and a saxophone.
- an air jet has **aim**, how the stream sits across the edge, which is what a flute player's embouchure controls.

## The tube

- **bore** - cylindrical or conical. A cylinder closed at one end produces only odd harmonics, which is why a clarinet sounds hollow and a saxophone does not, and it falls straight out of the model.
- **body** and **bell** - the shape of the end.
- **loss** - stated per round trip, so it means the same at any length.
- **fingering**, **below** and **answer** - the tone-hole lattice. The tube is modelled **below the note**: the holes are open past the first one, and where the wave turns round is what the note is.
- **fork** - the cross-fingerings, which on a real instrument give you the notes between and sound slightly different from the ones that are not.

## Blowing it

**pressure** and **breath** as on Brazen, with **jet** and **aim** for the flute family, and **tongue** for articulation.

## Using it well

**Pressure decides the register.** Overblow a clarinet and it jumps a twelfth, not an octave - because of the odd harmonics - and this model does that.

**The breath noise is most of the realism.** A flute with none is a sine.

**Tongue between notes, not before them.** Articulation is what stops a phrase sounding like one long note with pitch changes.
