# Timber

> Modelled woodwinds: reeds and flutes.

Timber is the woodwind version of Brazen: a reed vibrating against a mouthpiece,
or a jet of air blown across an edge, feeding a tube with holes in it.

## The family

**family** picks the kind of instrument:

- **reed** - clarinet and saxophone.
- **double** - a double reed, oboe and bassoon.
- **air** - a jet of air, the flute. **aim** sets how the air hits the edge,
  like a flute player's lips.

## The tube

- **shape** - the bore, a cylinder or a cone. A cylinder gives the hollow
  clarinet sound and a cone sounds more like a sax.
- **body** and **bell** - the shape of the end.
- **loss** - how much energy the tube loses.
- **fingering**, **below** and **answer** - the tone holes, including the part of
  the tube past the note you're playing.
- **forked** - cross-fingerings, which sound a little different from normal
  fingerings like on a real instrument.

## Blowing

**pressure** and **breath** work like Brazen's. The flutes add **jet** and
**aim**, and **tongue** adds articulation. **flutter** rolls the tongue at its
**rate**: on a reed it taps the reed shut, on a flute it breaks up the air jet.

## Swell

The **swell** group on the **mouth** page moves the breath over the start of
each note. Turn **amount** up and the breath starts low and rises to the set
pressure, so the note grows out of nothing at 1. Turn it down and the breath
starts above the pressure and falls back to it, an accent. **time** is how
long it takes. A slurred note carries on the swell it joined.

## Macros

The **play** page has eight macro knobs. A patch can name them (Blow, Breathy,
etc.) and the names show on the knobs, on automation lanes and in the
controller mapping list. They sit at zero when a patch loads, which is the
patch as it was made.

The **macros** page sets what each one does. A macro has four routes, a
destination (**to**) and an amount (**by**, from -1 to 1) for each. Turning the
knob moves the destination by knob times amount, across that control's whole
range at 1. Pitch moves the whole instrument, 24 semitones at 1. The family,
shape, vent, filter type and mode switches and the tuning steps can't be
reached. A change to the swell, glide, key noise or velocity is heard from the
next note. Tap a macro's name to rename it.

## Tips

- Pressure decides the register. Overblow a clarinet and it jumps up a twelfth
  instead of an octave, like the real one.
- The breath noise is a big part of the realism.
- Use **tongue** between notes so a phrase doesn't sound like one long note.
