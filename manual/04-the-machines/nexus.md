# Nexus

> A modular whose blocks are this app's own instruments, and whose patch is text.

Nexus is the machine for building a machine. Its blocks are not generic oscillators and filters - they are **the app's own instruments**, so a Reflux filter, a Trinity oscillator or a Resonance mode bank can be wired into a graph together.

## The patch is text

A Nexus patch is written out rather than drawn: a list of blocks and a list of connections, in a form you can read, copy, send to somebody and keep in a comment. It opens on a screen of its own because a graph needs room.

Knobs are parameters in the ordinary sense - so anything in a Nexus patch can be automated, mapped to a controller and recorded, exactly as any other machine's controls can.

## The macros

Eight of them, **macro1** to **macro8**, plus **morph**. A patch decides what they reach; the panel always shows the same eight. That means a complicated graph can present three useful controls, and the patch author decides which three.

## Blocks worth knowing

Alongside the instrument blocks there is an **audio input** block, which is how anything coming into the phone can be routed through a graph - and the only way, today, to put a live signal through a rack's insert effects.

## Using it well

**Build small and name your macros.** A graph with thirty blocks and no macros is one nobody can play, including you next week.

**Morph is the performance control.** Wire it to the two or three things that change the patch's character and leave everything else alone.

**It is not a beginner's machine and it does not pretend to be.** Everything Nexus can do, one of the other nineteen does more directly - what it offers is the combinations none of them cover.
