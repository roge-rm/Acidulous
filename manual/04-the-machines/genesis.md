# Genesis

> The big drum box: circuit drift, and a bus compressor with the kick wired to its side chain.

Genesis is the other drum machine - the large one, after the boxes that defined house and techno. Twelve voices, more weight than Hexbeat, and two ideas that Hexbeat does not have.

## The two ideas

**Circuit drift.** Real analogue drum machines are never quite the same twice: components warm up and every hit is a little different. **drift** is that, and a small amount of it is the difference between a machine and a loop.

**A bus with the kick on its side chain.** The whole kit runs through a compressor - **comp**, **compattack**, **comprelease** - and **duck** wires the kick into it, so every kick pulls the rest of the kit down and lets it back. That pumping is not an effect added afterwards here; it is part of the instrument, which is how those records were made.

## The kit

- **kick** - tune, decay, level, **sweep**, **punch**, **click** and **drive**. The sweep is the long pitch fall that makes this kick this kick; the click is the attack transient; the drive is where it stops being polite.
- **snare**, **clap** with its spread and tone, **rim**.
- **toms**, **hats** with a shared tone and tune and separate open and closed decays.
- **crash** and **ride**, the ride with its own **bell**.
- **cowbell** - tune, decay, level, and yes.

## Using it well

**Set the duck before you set the levels.** How much the kit moves under the kick changes how loud everything else needs to be, so doing it the other way round means doing it twice.

**The kick's drive is a mix control.** It adds harmonics that a small speaker can reproduce, so a kick that disappears on a phone usually wants drive rather than level.

**A little drift, not a lot.** Enough that two bars are not identical; not so much that the hats sound broken.
