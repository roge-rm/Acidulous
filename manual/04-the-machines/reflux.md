# Reflux

> The acid bass: one oscillator, one filter that screams, and a line you play rather than program.

Reflux is the machine this app is named around. One oscillator, one filter, one envelope - and the whole of it is arranged so that a bassline is *performed* into the sequencer rather than assembled in it.

## The idea

The famous silver box had no velocity and no legato. It had **accent** and **slide**, and every acid line ever written is those two switches used well. Reflux keeps both, and makes them things you already know how to do:

- **Accent is velocity.** Play a note harder and it is accented - louder, brighter, and with a sharper envelope into the filter.
- **Slide is legato.** Overlap two notes and the second slides into the first's pitch instead of retriggering. Let go before the next and it plucks.

So a line is played in on the keyboard, or drawn with overlaps in the roll, and it behaves the way the records do.

## The controls

- **osc** - the waveform, from saw through to square, and a sub an octave below. The saw is the classic; the square is the other half of the vocabulary.
- **filter** - cutoff and resonance, plus the envelope amount and its decay. This is the instrument: almost everything anybody plays on one of these is a cutoff sweep with resonance up.
- **env** - one decay-shaped envelope, and how much of it reaches the filter. Short decays give the plucked sound; long ones give the rubbery one.
- **accent** - how much an accented note adds, to both level and filter. At nothing, velocity does nothing and you have a machine with no dynamics.
- **glide** - how long a slide takes. Long glides over a fast line stop sounding like slides and start sounding like a portamento pad, which is occasionally what you want.
- **drive** - the output stage, which is where the scream comes from. It scales against the level the signal reaches rather than against full scale, so a quiet patch drives the same as a loud one.

## Using it well

**Resonance and drive together are the sound.** Resonance alone thins the bass out; drive alone thickens it without the whistle. Most classic patches have both up and the cutoff moving.

**Automate the cutoff rather than turning it.** The strip under the grid records a knob as you move it, so a sixteen-bar filter sweep is one pass of the recording rather than a line you have to draw.

**Two Refluxes is the other classic trick** - one plucked and one slid, panned apart, playing the same clip with the second's cutoff a little different.

*Reflux was called Subvert in earlier releases. Songs and patches saved then open as Reflux.*
