# Resonance

> Eight struck objects that ring - and hear each other.

Resonance is modal percussion. Each of its eight pads is an object described by its **modes** - the frequencies it rings at and how long each one lasts - and playing it is striking that object. Nothing is sampled and nothing is a filtered noise burst; the ringing is the instrument.

## What a pad is

- **modes** - how many partials the object has. Few is a woodblock, many is a bell.
- **shape** - membrane, bar, plate, tube, bowl or metal, which sets the *ratios* between the modes. A bar's partials are nothing like a drum head's, and that difference is most of what tells them apart.
- **inharm** - stretches those ratios away from where the shape put them.
- **decay** and **damp** - how long it rings, and whether the top goes first.
- **hard** - how hard the striker is, which is really how much high end goes in.
- **hit** - **where** you strike it. This is the control worth knowing: a mode has nodes, and striking at a node does not excite that mode at all. Moving `hit` silences and revives whole partials, which is why the same object struck in two places is two instruments.
- **bend** and **bendtime** - a pitch fall after the strike, which is what a tom is.
- **drive**, **pan**, **level** and **couple**.

## The coupling

**coupling** is how much the pads ring into each other. Strike one and the others answer, quietly, at their own frequencies - which is what a rack of real objects in one room does, and what makes a kit built here sound like one place rather than eight.

**humanise** varies the strike a little each time.

## Using it well

**Start with `hit` and `shape`, not with tune.** They decide what the object *is*; tuning it comes after.

**Coupling is loud.** A little is a room; a lot is feedback with a pitch.

**Long decays plus the grid is a tuned percussion instrument** - write a melody on the pads and let them overlap.
